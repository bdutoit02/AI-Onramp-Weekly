Week ending 9 October 2026

Maths is the big topic for the week, with OpenAI's release of 90 newly-solved problems from a list of 500 major outstanding questions. The result is real and significant, and the implications are profound. In the readings here I try to focus on understanding the technical aspects of exactly what was achieved, and how, but we also cover some of the inevitable noise and hot takes. Zvi provides his usual extensive coverage - lots of summaries and links to the most important sites, papers and reactions. Then another review-type article from AI Daily Brief. Lastly a nice summary of thoughts by many of the leading mathematicians.

Then I do a couple of things that I am experimenting with. First I give the link to the OpenAI repository, where they show all the results in a few different ways: the paper in standard mathematical format, some examples of the reasoning traces logged by the AI agents as they go about trying to solve the problem, and then some examples of the Lean proofs which are a critical part of the success of gen AI in making these inroads into (very) serious mathematics. To help you through the repository, I asked Claude to choose a few of the relatively simpler problems (they are not, of course, remotely simple), and guide a reader through the different aspects of the solution and the solution process. For this see the document "Tour". (Warning, this document is not foolproof and you may need to click around a bit to get to what you need to see. I will probably try to update this later in a nicer format)

Then, another experiment, the interaction between the mathematical solution checker Lean and agentic generative AI is a key element in the recent rapid progress by AI in higher mathematics. I discussed this with Claude, in some detail, and then got Claude to write up a summary of our main argument. You may find it useful.

Lastly for a little variation, an interesting discussion on the excellent Latent Space pod with the leaders of Periodic Labs, one of the most interesting labs working on the application of AI to physics, in this case specifically materials science.

Week ending 18 September 2026

The week was dominated by escalating safety concerns around the capabilities of frontier models, especially in the light of the possibility of imminent recursive self-improvement. Naturally this led into some very hot takes on either side of the regulation debate. These are some useful readings. Also: OpenAI solved a Millenium prizes problem in mathematics. Millenium prizes are a very big deal mathematically. But there was plenty of drama!
