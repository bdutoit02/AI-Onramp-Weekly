Week ending 9 October 2026

This week: AI and mathematics

Maths is the big topic for the week, with OpenAI's release of 90 newly-solved problems from a list of 500 major outstanding questions. The result is real and significant, and the implications are profound. In the readings here I try to focus on understanding the technical aspects of exactly what was achieved, and how — but we also cover some of the inevitable noise and hot takes.

The overviews

  • Zvi provides his usual extensive coverage — lots of summaries and links to the most important sites, papers and reactions.
  • AI Daily Brief offers another review-type article (again with a number of key links, which I won't reproduce here).
  • Lastly, a nice summary of thoughts from many of the leading mathematicians (you may want to update the date in the URL).

The source: OpenAI's repository

Then I give the link to the OpenAI repository, where OpenAI show all the results in a few different ways:

  • the paper in standard mathematical format
  • some examples of the reasoning traces logged by the AI agents as they go about trying to solve the problems
  • some examples of the Lean proofs, which are a critical part of the success of gen AI in making these inroads into (very) serious mathematics

💡 New to GitHub? GitHub is where developers share code (in "code repositories"), but it is also very popular for publishing results that involve code alongside other materials. If you are not familiar with GitHub, or with the nature of mathematical proofs in AI systems, drop the link into a decent AI and ask it to guide you through it.

An experiment: Lean meets agentic AI

The interaction between the mathematical solution checker Lean and agentic generative AI is a key element in the recent rapid progress by AI in higher mathematics. I discussed this with Claude, in some detail, and then got Claude to write up a summary of our main argument ("How Lean plus gen AI combine to provide a formidable mathematical research tool"). You may find it useful.

For a little variation: Periodic Labs on Latent Space

An interesting discussion on the excellent Latent Space pod with the leaders of Periodic Labs, one of the most interesting labs working on the application of AI to physics — in this case specifically materials science.

Week ending 18 September 2026

The week was dominated by escalating safety concerns around the capabilities of frontier models, especially in the light of the possibility of imminent recursive self-improvement. Naturally this led into some very hot takes on either side of the regulation debate. These are some useful readings. Also: OpenAI solved a Millenium prizes problem in mathematics.
