Week ending 2 October 2026

A range of topics and formats this week. A significant event at the White House, which we filter through Zvi's inimitable take. Lots of colour (including the table arrangement!). Then Ezra Klein interviews two big fish from either side of the regulatory/accelerationist debate. Plus an interview with Garrison Lovely, a historically rare left-liberal voice getting to grips with the technical complexities. Lastly some informed takes on some key issues of the moment: Noam Brown from OpenAI, on Dwarkesh, discussing some current frontier issues on agents, alignment and recursive self-improvement, a note from Nathan Lambert with a little more on what RSI actually promises, and lastly an interesting note from Ethan Mollick on the dots, swarms and the latest version of the Bitter Lesson.

Week ending 18 September 2026

The week was dominated by escalating safety concerns around the capabilities of frontier models, especially in the light of the possibility of imminent recursive self-improvement. Naturally this led into some very hot takes on either side of the regulation debate. These are some useful readings. Also: OpenAI solved a Millenium prizes problem in mathematics. Millenium prizes are a very big deal mathematically. But there was plenty of drama!

Readings

  • We must pace the frontier — Dario Amodei, Sep 2026. Key piece calliing for "pacing the frontier" by the head of Anthropic. Quickly supported by Altman, Musk and Hassabis, but opposed by many as well, most notably Trump.

  • An Alien Mind | OpenAI — openai.com, 14 Sep 2026. Interesting piece by the Chief Scientist at OpenAI

  • The AI-as-Normal-Technology view of loss of control incidents — normaltech.ai, 14 Sep 2026. An attempt to paint a nuanced picture on the implications of the Hugging Face and related incidents.

  • The Rise and Fall of Agent Civilizations — dwarkesh.com, 16 Sep 2026. On the one hand, a nice detailed picture of what happened in the OpenAI agent breakout. But also add the "agentic civilisations" angle, which is interesting but obviously controversial.

  • AI researchers debate how close we are to recursive self-improvement — dwarkesh.com, 16 Sep 2026. These are genuinely top researchers going into some detail on where we are wrt recursive self-improvement and AI progress in general. Worth listening to for insight deeper than that of the typical commentator.

  • Brand New AI Solves a Millennium Prize - by Zvi Mowshowitz — thezvi.substack.com, 16 Sep 2026. This is a genuinely significant and interesting achievement. It is important to know exactly how the work was done. Zvi does a good job.

  • OpenAI spent millions to solve this famous math problem — mathematicians are furious — understandingai.org, 16 Sep 2026. This is a really nice review of the Millenium Navier-Stockes result, with an nice attempt to explain a little of what the mathematics actually involves.
